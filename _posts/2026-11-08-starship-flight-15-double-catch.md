SpaceX has set November 13 for Starship Flight 15, a test that will try to catch both the upper stage and the Super Heavy booster at Starbase. The company says a 75-minute window opens at 4:45 p.m. CT, pending regulatory approval. If the attempt works, it will be the first time an orbital launch system has been fully recovered.

The flight is the next step in SpaceX's plan to make Starship reusable in the way Falcon 9 became reusable. The Starship catch works differently from a Falcon 9 landing. Falcon 9 lands a booster on legs. Starship plans to catch both halves of the stack with the launch tower. ## What is SpaceX attempting on Flight 15?

SpaceX will try to launch Starship, deploy satellites, bring the ship back through the atmosphere, and catch it with the tower at Starbase. The Super Heavy booster will attempt the same return to the launch site after stage separation. Both catches are planned for the same flight.

The company calls this the first full recovery of an orbital launch system. Until now, Starship flights ended with a controlled splashdown in the ocean. Flight 14 on September 28 was the vehicle's first orbital mission, and it proved the ship could reenter and reach a target point at sea. Flight 15 moves the endpoint from water to a tower.

The flight also carries a commercial payload. SpaceX says the ship will deploy 26 Starlink V3 satellites, making this a test of orbital operations as well as reuse.

## Why is catching the ship harder than catching the booster?

The booster returns on a predictable arc shortly after separation. The ship comes back after more than 22 hours in orbit, moving at orbital speed. To reach Starbase, it has to fly over land.

SpaceX says the trajectory will pass over northern Mexico and southern Texas. That is a change from earlier flights, which kept reentry over unpopulated ocean areas. The company says it has built in several chances to end the mission safely if the vehicle is not healthy before the final approach.

The catch itself depends on the tower arms, the vehicle's guidance and the condition of the ship after reentry. SpaceX says it will only commit to the return-to-site attempt if the systems needed for reentry, the landing burn and the tower approach are working.

The schedule is dynamic. SpaceX says launch dates and times can change, and the company has moved Starship flights before. Treat the date as a target, not a guarantee.

## What does the Super Heavy catch involve?

The Super Heavy catch starts with a launch, a stage separation and a boostback burn. The booster then returns to the launch site. This is the first catch attempt for a V3 booster. SpaceX caught three V1 boosters on earlier tests, and two of those flew again.

A booster catch is now routine enough that SpaceX describes it as a primary objective. The harder part is doing it with the newer vehicle while the ship is also coming home. A problem with the booster would change the mission's outcome, though the ship's catch could still proceed.

## What would a successful flight prove?

A double catch would show that both halves of Starship can return to the pad. That matters for Starlink V3, because the constellation needs frequent, heavy launches. It also gives SpaceX its first look at how much work a recovered ship needs before it can fly again.

The flight will not answer every question about reuse. SpaceX still has to show that a recovered ship can be turned around quickly, and that the tower can handle a regular cadence of catches. Flight 15 is the first data point, not the finished system.

## What could go wrong?

Plenty. The ship could fail during reentry. The tower could miss the catch. Weather could delay the launch. Regulators could require changes before the flight. SpaceX can also choose to abort the return and splash down if the vehicle is not within the limits for a catch.

The company has been careful to describe the catch as conditional. That is normal for a first attempt. A splashdown would still give engineers data, and it would not mean the program failed. It would mean the next flight has more work to do.

## How to watch Starship Flight 15

SpaceX plans a live webcast starting about 30 minutes before liftoff on its website and on X. The company says coverage will continue through the ship catch, with live views from orbit expected during the flight.

If you are watching from outside the United States, check the local time for 4:45 p.m. CT. The window is short, and the launch can slip within it. SpaceX usually confirms any new time on its launch page and social accounts.
